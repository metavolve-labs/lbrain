# Why things are the way they are

A successor who does not know why a decision was made will either preserve it
past its usefulness or reverse it and rediscover the reason the hard way. The
reason is the asset; the decision is only its residue.

## Questions

- What is the current arrangement here, and what was rejected to arrive at it?
- What did you know at the time that made the rejected option worse? Is that
  still true?
- Which decisions were made under a constraint that has since lifted — a budget,
  a vendor contract, a person, a deadline?
- What looks like a deliberate choice but is actually an accident nobody has had
  reason to revisit?
- Where did you change your mind, and what changed it?
- What would you do differently starting today, and what is stopping you?

## The record this produces

One `decision` record per decision that would be expensive to reverse blindly:
what was chosen, what was rejected, the reason, and the condition under which the
reason stops holding. That last line is what lets a successor reopen the decision
honestly instead of guessing.
