# What belongs to the organisation, and what belongs to you

Answer this **before** the corpus grows, not at the point of departure. A
boundary drawn while leaving is drawn under pressure by someone with an interest
in where it lands, and neither side trusts it.

A corpus built alongside a role contains three different things, and only the
first is straightforwardly the organisation's:

1. **The work** — decisions, state, commitments, process. Made on the
   organisation's time, about its business.
2. **The personal** — your own reasoning, drafts, judgements about people,
   candid notes you wrote to think rather than to publish.
3. **Third parties** — colleagues, clients and counterparts who never agreed to
   appear in anyone's records, and whose appearance is a disclosure the moment
   the corpus changes hands.

## Questions

- Which of your record types are unambiguously the organisation's, and which are
  yours? Write the list before you need it.
- What would you not want a successor to read, and is that because it is
  personal, because it is unkind, or because it is unfinished?
- Who reviews the corpus before it transfers, against what standard, and who
  arbitrates a disagreement about a specific record?
- What is the rule for records that name a person who is not party to the
  handover?
- Which records should transfer sealed — available for audit, not for reading?
- Is there a step at which the outgoing person marks records as personal, and is
  that step available *before* a departure is announced?

## The record this produces

One `boundary` record, written early and reviewed when the role changes hands:
which record types transfer, which stay, which transfer sealed, and who decides a
contested case. This engine can enforce a boundary that has been drawn — see the
disclosure classes — but it cannot draw one, and it should not be the party that
decides where the line goes.
